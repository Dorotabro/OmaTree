 OmaTree: notes that branch the way your thinking does.

      You start documenting one thing, and three questions later you're four levels deep in something you never planned to look at. Most note apps make you pay for that: folders to file things into, a sidebar full of controls, a toolbar you never touch. OmaTree makes it cheap. You write, press Ctrl+N or Ctrl+Shift+N, and the rabbit hole gets its own note, right under the one that spawned it. Nothing gets lost, because the tree is the trail.

      What it's built around, going by what we've made so far:

      - One idea, two panes. A tree on the left, the selected note on the right. Every node is a note, so there are no folders or note types to think about.
      - Quiet by default. There is no toolbar and no ribbon, and the only controls are a few small buttons and one File menu. It's meant to feel like a small terminal-era tool.
      - It never loses your work. Autosave is silent. Deletes go to Trash, and big changes take recovery checkpoints, so a bad drag or an accidental delete can be undone.
      - Your notes stay yours. A notebook is one file on your disk. There's no account, no cloud and no telemetry, and the Markdown preview won't fetch a thing from the internet.
      - Fast to move around. Drag rows to reorganize, Alt+Up and Alt+Down to reorder, and Ctrl+F to jump anywhere. The keyboard comes first.
      - Looks like it belongs. On Omarchy it picks up your active theme live. Elsewhere it follows your system's light or dark setting.

      The honest tagline is probably the one you already said: beautifully and stupidly simple. CherryTree proved the tree-of-notes idea is right, and OmaTree's pitch is keeping the idea and leaving the clutter behind.

      A caution on the pitch: I'd only say what is true today. It's a young app. It runs on Linux and has only been exercised on Omarchy. It has no sync, no images and no plugins, and it should stay that way, since those absences are part of the selling point.
